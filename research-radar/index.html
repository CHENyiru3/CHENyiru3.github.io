Computational #1 9.0

Systematic benchmarking of zero-shot utility and robustness in single-cell transcriptomic foundation models

bioRxiv Published 2026-06-23 preprint DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.18.733285

Authors: Liu, T., Feng, T., Pan, X., Chen, Y., Ren, L., Ye, X., Sakurai, T., Lin, H., Zhang, Y.

single-cell foundation models benchmarking zero-shot learning transcriptomics representation learning model robustness

Summary: Systematically evaluated single-cell transcriptomic representations in zero-shot settings across 20 methods, 6 downstream tasks, and 1,607 datasets comprising nearly 21.8 million cells. The large-scale analysis reveals a decoupling between utility and robustness: methods ranking highly on standard benchmarks often show marked instability under shifts in dataset structure, and no single model performs uniformly well across tasks. Critically, classical statistical representations based on highly variable genes remain competitive under zero-shot conditions, defining the practical boundaries of zero-shot use in single-cell foundation models.

Why it matters: This is the most comprehensive zero-shot benchmark of single-cell foundation models to date, providing a much-needed reality check on the current generation of scFMs. The finding that simple HVG-based representations remain competitive has immediate practical implications for researchers choosing tools for single-cell analysis, and the decision framework for representation selection will guide both users and model developers.

Why for Yiru: Directly informs tool selection for spatial omics and single-cell computational immunology analyses — the benchmark includes tasks relevant to cell type annotation, batch integration, and embedding evaluation that are core to TME and digital twin workflows.

Computational #2 8.5

VCBench: A Multi-Dimensional Benchmark for Single-Cell Foundation Models

bioRxiv Published 2026-06-23 preprint DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.18.733146

Authors: Weidener, L. S., Brkic, M., Jovanovic, M., Ulgac, E., Meduri, A.

single-cell foundation models virtual cell benchmarking gene regulatory networks cross-species perturbation prediction

Summary: Introduces VCBench, a benchmark synthesizing four independent virtual-cell frameworks into seven capability dimensions: perturbation response prediction, cross-species universality, gene regulatory network inference, modality integration, temporal dynamics, multi-scale integration, and in silico experimentation. Simple linear baselines match or exceed every foundation model on four of five testable dimensions, with only TranscriptFormer showing meaningful improvement on cross-modal RNA-to-protein prediction. The benchmark also exposes a critical tradeoff: the architectural choice enabling TranscriptFormer's cross-modal advantage simultaneously causes spectral collapse that destroys temporal-ordering performance.

Why it matters: VCBench provides the most rigorous multi-dimensional evaluation framework for single-cell foundation models, moving beyond single-task benchmarks that obscure model limitations. The finding that foundation models largely fail to outperform simple baselines challenges the narrative of rapid progress and establishes a higher bar for claiming meaningful advances.

Why for Yiru: The virtual cell evaluation dimensions — especially perturbation response, GRN inference, and temporal dynamics — are directly relevant to building computational models of cellular systems for digital twin AI applications in immunology and spatial omics.

Computational #3 8.0

Biophysical modeling for accurate T cell specificity prediction of viral and tumor antigens

Nature Communications Published 2026-06-23 Research Article DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-74236-0

Authors: George, J. T.

T cell receptor specificity prediction computational immunology TCR-pMHC machine learning cancer immunotherapy

Summary: Develops a computational approach that combines TCR primary sequences with TCR-pMHC structural features to predict T cell specificity from clinical samples. The prediction model, trained on both sequence and structural data, was validated on hematopoietic stem cell transplant and other clinical cohorts, demonstrating accurate identification of T cells targeting viral and tumor antigens.

Why it matters: TCR specificity prediction remains one of the hardest problems in computational immunology due to the enormous combinatorial diversity of peptide-MHC-TCR interactions. This work bridges sequence-based and structure-based approaches, offering a practical tool for clinical applications including monitoring transplant rejection and identifying tumor-reactive T cells.

Why for Yiru: TCR specificity prediction is a cornerstone technology for computational immunology and digital twin immune modeling — accurate prediction of which antigens a T cell recognizes enables in silico simulation of immune responses and neoantigen prioritization.

Computational #4 7.5

MIND: multimodal integration with neighbourhood-aware distributions

Nature Communications Published 2026-06-24 Research Article DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-74413-1

Authors: Yau, C.

multi-omics integration missing data patient embeddings machine learning multimodal learning precision medicine

Summary: Proposes MIND, a platform that learns patient-specific embeddings from incomplete multi-omics datasets. Current multi-omics integration methods rely on assumptions that can cause information distortion, particularly when data is missing across modalities. MIND addresses this by learning neighbourhood-aware distributions that handle missingness and heterogeneity inherent in real-world multi-omics data without distorting biological signals.

Why it matters: Missing data is the rule rather than the exception in multi-omics studies, yet most integration methods either discard incomplete samples or rely on imputation strategies that introduce bias. MIND's ability to learn robust embeddings from incomplete data could unlock analyses of large-scale biobank and clinical cohorts where complete multi-omics profiles are rarely available.

Why for Yiru: Multi-omics integration with principled missing-data handling is essential for spatial omics analyses that combine transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics data, and for building comprehensive patient-specific digital twin models from heterogeneous clinical data.

antibody design protein engineering epitope targeting deep learning CDR design computational immunology

Why it matters: De novo antibody design targeting specific epitopes has been a grand challenge in computational biology. Why for Yiru: Epitope-targeted antibody design tools can enhance spatial omics probe development and enable computational design of antibodies for multiplexed tissue imaging, as well as informing in silico antibody repertoire analysis in computational immunology.

Cuproptosis-immunity crosstalk informs strategy to overcome immunotherapy resistance

Cell Published 2026-06-22 Research Article DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2026.05.036

Authors: Lei, G., Lu, Z., Xu, Z., Braun, C., Huo, D., Gao, J., Tan, L., Hong, T., Wu, S., Sun, M., Zhao, X., Li, Q., Chen, X., Yan, Y., Lee, H., Mao, C., Zhuang, L., Ku, L.-T., Puebla, N., Barsoumian, H., Yao, J., Hong, L., Zhang, J., Tran, H., Lee, J.-K. J., Gibbons, D., Vaporciyan, A., Heymach, J., Lin, C., Gottlieb, E., You, M. J., Welsh, J. W., Lin, S. H., Zang, X., Li, Z., Gan, B.

cuproptosis immunotherapy resistance PD-L1 blockade antitumor immunity CD8 T cells IFN-gamma tumor microenvironment

Summary: Uncovers a reciprocal circuit linking cuproptosis (copper-dependent cell death) and antitumor immunity. CD8+ T cell-derived IFN-γ enhances tumor cell susceptibility to cuproptosis, and engaging this axis with PD-L1 blockade amplifies tumor cell killing. This mechanistic connection between copper metabolism and immune-mediated cell death provides a strategy to overcome immunotherapy resistance.

Why it matters: This study identifies an entirely new axis connecting metal-dependent cell death to adaptive immunity, opening a novel therapeutic avenue for patients who fail to respond to checkpoint blockade. The finding that IFN-γ directly primes tumor cells for cuproptosis suggests that combining copper ionophores with immunotherapy could convert resistant tumors into responsive ones — a concept with immediate translational potential.

Why for Yiru: The mechanistic link between T cell-derived cytokines and tumor cell death modality is directly relevant to computational modeling of tumor-immune interactions in the TME and could inform digital twin simulations of immunotherapy combination strategies.

Biomedicine #2 8.0

Mutant KRAS peptide vaccine with dual checkpoint blockade in metastatic colorectal cancer: a phase I trial

Nature Communications Published 2026-06-23 Research Article DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-74711-8

Authors: Zaidi, N.

KRAS vaccine colorectal cancer immunotherapy checkpoint blockade phase I trial neoantigen

Summary: Reports a phase I clinical trial evaluating mKRAS-VAX, a mutant KRAS peptide vaccine, in combination with nivolumab (anti-PD-1) and ipilimumab (anti-CTLA-4) in heavily pretreated patients with metastatic mismatch repair proficient/microsatellite stable (MMRp/MSS) colorectal cancer. This patient population typically fails to respond to immune checkpoint inhibitors alone, making them a critical unmet need group.

Why it matters: MMRp/MSS colorectal cancer represents the majority of CRC cases and has been largely refractory to immunotherapy. A successful KRAS-targeted vaccine strategy could transform the treatment landscape for these patients. The combination of a personalized neoantigen vaccine with dual checkpoint blockade represents a rational strategy to prime and sustain anti-tumor T cell responses.

Why for Yiru: Clinical trial data combining neoantigen vaccines with checkpoint blockade provides valuable ground truth for computational models of cancer vaccine efficacy and tumor-immune dynamics, directly informing digital twin immunotherapy simulations.

Biomedicine #3 7.0

Costimulation drives CAR-T cell division fate

Nature Immunology Published 2026-06-22 News & Views DOI: 10.1038/s41590-026-02559-7

Authors: Adeshakin, A. O., Gottschalk, S.

CAR-T cells asymmetric cell division costimulatory domains 4-1BB CD28 immunotherapy

Summary: Discusses recent findings showing that asymmetric cell division acts as an early program selector in CAR-T cells. The data explain why 4-1BBζ CAR-T cells preferentially generate durable memory progeny, whereas CD28ζ CAR-T cells skew toward short-lived effector fates, providing a mechanistic basis for differential CAR-T cell persistence observed clinically.

Why it matters: Understanding the molecular basis for why different CAR costimulatory domains produce distinct clinical outcomes has been a central question in CAR-T biology. This mechanistic insight into asymmetric cell division as a fate determinant could guide rational design of next-generation CAR constructs with optimized persistence and effector function.

Why for Yiru: CAR-T cell fate programming through asymmetric division is a key parameter for computational models of adoptive cell therapy dynamics and could be incorporated into digital twin simulations of CAR-T treatment responses.

Biological aging and generational shifts in early-onset cancer risk

Nature Medicine Published 2026-06-22 Research Article DOI: 10.1038/s41591-026-04448-w

Authors: Cao, Y.

biological aging early-onset cancer epidemiology cancer risk population health aging biomarkers

Summary: Population cohort analyses reveal that young adults exhibit earlier systemic and organ-specific biological aging compared to older adults born decades earlier. This accelerated biological aging was associated with increased risk of early-onset cancer, suggesting that generational shifts in aging biology may underlie the rising incidence of cancer in younger populations.

Why it matters: The rising incidence of early-onset cancers has been a growing public health concern with unclear etiology. This study provides epidemiological evidence linking accelerated biological aging to this trend, suggesting that biomarkers of biological age could be used to identify high-risk individuals for early cancer screening.

Why for Yiru: Incorporating biological aging metrics into multi-scale digital twin models could improve personalized cancer risk prediction and inform the design of age-aware computational frameworks for precision oncology.

Mapping and engineering the human cell–cell interactome

Nature Biotechnology Published 2026-06-23 Review DOI: 10.1038/s41587-026-03177-2

Authors: Witte, O.

cell-cell interactions interactome systems biology protein engineering therapeutic targeting multicellular organization

Summary: Di Carlo and colleagues discuss the technologies required to systematically map and engineer the human cell–cell interactome. The review outlines the experimental and computational approaches needed to build a comprehensive atlas of cell-surface receptor–ligand interactions across all human cell types, and explores the therapeutic avenues such an atlas could unlock, including targeted protein therapeutics and synthetic cell–cell communication circuits.

Why it matters: A comprehensive cell–cell interactome atlas would be a foundational resource for cell biology, akin to the human genome sequence. Understanding the complete wiring diagram of cell–cell communication could transform drug target discovery, enable rational design of cell-based therapies, and provide a systems-level framework for understanding tissue organization and disease.

Why for Yiru: Cell–cell interaction mapping is directly foundational to spatial omics analysis pipelines and the construction of multi-cellular digital twin models that require accurate ligand–receptor interaction databases.

Field #2 6.0

EasyGrid: a versatile platform for automated cryo-EM sample preparation and quality control

Nature Methods Published 2026-06-23 Research Article DOI: 10.1038/s41592-026-03127-5

Authors: Papp, G.

cryo-EM automation sample preparation structural biology high-throughput cryo-ET

Summary: Presents EasyGrid, a fully automated and modular platform for high-throughput sample vitrification for cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) and cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET). The system integrates in-line plasma treatment of the sample support, microfluidic dispensing, blot-less sample spreading, jet-based vitrification, and automated grid quality control via light interferometry, addressing the sample preparation bottleneck that has limited cryo-EM throughput.

Why it matters: Sample preparation remains the primary bottleneck in cryo-EM workflows, with grid preparation being notoriously variable and labor-intensive. EasyGrid's fully automated, standardized approach could dramatically increase the throughput and reproducibility of structural biology studies, accelerating drug discovery and fundamental research.

Why for Yiru: Automated structural biology pipelines could eventually integrate with spatial omics for multi-scale molecular characterization, and the high-throughput nature of EasyGrid aligns with the scale of data generation needed for comprehensive digital twin models.

Field #3 5.5

Pseudouridine synthase PUS1 and initiation factor mtIF2 are human mitoribosomal small subunit assembly factors

Nature Communications Published 2026-06-24 Research Article DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-74700-x

Authors: Rorbach, J.

mitoribosome cryo-EM mitochondrial translation ribosome assembly PUS1 structural biology

Summary: Using cryo-electron microscopy, describes 12 distinct assembly states of the human mitoribosomal small subunit and identifies pseudouridine synthase PUS1 and mitochondrial translation initiation factor mtIF2 as critical mitoribosome assembly factors. This work provides the first detailed structural view of how the human mitochondrial ribosome is constructed, a process essential for cellular energy production.

Why it matters: Mitochondrial translation defects underlie a growing number of human diseases, yet the assembly pathway of the mitoribosome has remained poorly characterized. This structural dissection of the assembly process provides a framework for understanding disease mutations and identifies new potential therapeutic targets for mitochondrial disorders.

Why for Yiru: The cryo-EM structural biology workflow demonstrates the power of integrative structural approaches that could be combined with spatial omics data for multi-scale cellular modeling.

Field #4 5.0

RGS22 is a metazoa-specific radial spoke component required for coordinated ciliary beating

Nature Communications Published 2026-06-24 Research Article DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-74801-7

Authors: Zhang, Q.

cilia radial spoke RGS22 ciliopathy hydrocephalus cell biology

Summary: Identifies RGS22 as a metazoa-specific component of the ciliary radial spoke, a structure essential for coordinating ciliary motion. Knockout of Rgs22 in mice causes ciliary ultrastructural defects, impaired tissue-level coordination of ciliary beating, and postnatal hydrocephalus, demonstrating the critical role of this protein in ciliary function.

Why it matters: Ciliary dysfunction underlies a broad class of human genetic disorders (ciliopathies), yet the molecular composition of key ciliary structures like the radial spoke remains incompletely defined. The discovery of RGS22 adds a new component to this structure and links its loss to hydrocephalus, providing a new candidate gene for human ciliopathy screening.

Why for Yiru: Understanding tissue-level coordination mechanisms — here, how individual cilia coordinate their beating — provides conceptual insights into spatial organization principles that are relevant to modeling multicellular emergent behaviors in digital twin systems.

Field #5 4.5

Optimized R2 retroelement complexes for DNA insertion into plant genomes

Nature Biotechnology Published 2026-06-19 Research Article DOI: 10.1038/s41587-026-03197-y

Authors: Demirer, G. S.

retrotransposons plant genome engineering R2 elements gene insertion synthetic biology ribosomal DNA

Summary: Demonstrates the use of optimized R2 retrotransposon complexes to achieve efficient site-specific DNA integration into plant and crop 25S ribosomal DNA sites. This approach leverages the natural targeting mechanism of R2 retroelements to insert transgenes at defined genomic safe-harbor loci, offering a new tool for precise plant genome engineering without the need for double-strand breaks.

Why it matters: Current plant genome engineering relies heavily on CRISPR-based methods that create double-strand breaks and rely on host repair pathways, which can introduce unwanted mutations. R2 retrotransposon-based insertion offers a scarless, targeted alternative that could accelerate crop improvement and plant synthetic biology applications.

Why for Yiru: Programmable DNA insertion technologies using retrotransposons provide conceptual parallels to spatial barcoding and lineage tracing approaches, and the site-specific targeting mechanism could inspire new strategies for genomic recording in spatial omics.
